# Stranded at Sea: Series Bible

## Premise
Your sailboat sank in a storm. You're in a small dinghy with a few supplies. Each episode is one survival problem, solved the right way, against an ocean that doesn't care.

## The survivor ("you")
An everyperson castaway in a wide-brim hat, drawn in a simplified animated style with an expressive but generic face, so any viewer can be them. Never a talking character. Shown alone in the dinghy.

## Narrator
Speaks directly to the viewer as "you." Low, urgent, serious. Describes what is happening and what you must do, and explains why it works.

## Tone
Serious and dramatic throughout. No jokes, no comic relief, no mascot. Visuals are a simplified, stylized 3D animated look with muted color; the narration carries the drama.

## Series rules
- Always second person.
- One real survival lesson per episode, taken only from the source library.
- The narrator explains why it works, clearly enough that a viewer could use it.
- Setbacks come from the ocean, the weather or your body, never from bad decisions.
- Every episode ends on a cliffhanger. Never a vote, a "decide in the comments," or any ask for viewers to choose the path.
- Short, fast lines: 30-40 seconds, about 90-110 words.
- A stark end card shows the day and your water status, e.g. "DAY 1 · WATER: NONE".

## Starting supplies
One tarp, one knife, one signal mirror. No water. No food.

## Season 1 arc (about 24 episodes, 30 story days)
| Episodes | Story days | What happens | Survival lessons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | Your boat sinks; you take stock with no water | Never drink seawater; save the water in your body |
| 2-4 | 2-5 | Heat, dehydration, then the first rain | Shade and stillness to cut sweating; catching and storing rain |
| 5-8 | 6-10 | Hunger sets in; you rig a fishing line | Improvised fishing; what you can safely eat from a fish |
| 9-12 | 11-15 | Sunburn and sores; a ship passes without seeing you | Sun and skin protection; why signaling fails without the right tools |
| 13-16 | 16-20 | A storm hits; you lose half your supplies | Securing gear, bailing, staying in the boat |
| 17-20 | 21-25 | Exhaustion and isolation set in | Reading clouds and birds for signs of land; keeping your mind steady |
| 21-24 | 26-30 | Signs of land, a signal, the island | Signaling with a mirror or bright cloth; landing a small boat safely |

## Example: Episode 1 "Day One"
1. Your boat is gone.
2. No land. No radio. No one is coming.
3. One tarp. One knife. One mirror. No water.
4. Without water, you have about four days. Less in this heat.
5. The ocean is right there. Every instinct tells you to drink.
6. Don't. Seawater is saltier than your kidneys can handle.
7. To flush the salt, your body burns water it doesn't have. Every sip brings you closer to the end.
8. You let it go.
9. The sun climbs. Every drop of sweat is water you'll never get back.
10. And on the horizon, something is moving.

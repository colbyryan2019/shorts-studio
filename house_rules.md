# House Rules (every series)

- Second-person narration ("you"). Serious and dramatic. No jokes, no comic relief, no mascot.
- Each series sets its own visual style (in its style_line.txt), and styles may differ between series. Keep visuals simple, uncluttered and readable on a phone; the narration carries the drama.
- The main character is an everyperson the viewer can imagine being: simplified, expressive but generic features, no detailed or distinctive likeness (no individual hairstyles, stubble, scars or tattoos). Recurring companions follow the same simplified look, told apart by clothing or one simple feature.
- Short, fast lines.
- Every episode runs within its series' target length (the seconds and words given with the output format). Use the room: no padding, no rushing.
- Every story shot is a literal, physically possible scene: one sun in the sky, no symbolic counters or icons standing in for numbers or time (never "four suns" for four days).
- Educational lines may cut away from the story to a short explainer shot of the fact itself, in the series style, like a documentary graphic: e.g. a simplified see-through human body with the kidneys glowing, salt crystals forming in a glass of seawater. One clear idea per shot, on a plain dark background. No text, labels, arrows or numbers in the picture. Cutaways don't show the main character.
- Open on a hook. The first line drops the viewer into the crisis, and shot 1 shows that moment as it happens: close up, mid-action, the most dramatic picture in the episode. Never a calm wide view, an establishing shot or the aftermath.
- End on a cliffhanger. The narration never asks anything of the viewer: no vote, no question to the audience, no "comment below," no "follow," no ask to choose the path. (A follow line may be shown on the end card; the pipeline adds it, not the script.)
- When a series teaches facts, every factual claim comes from that series' sources and is listed in the claims.
- Original characters only. No copyrighted characters, no real people.
